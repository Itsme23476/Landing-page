AI Assistant for PC: What to Install and What Your PC Needs

November 19, 2026·8 min read

Quick answer

For most people, the right AI assistant for a PC is decided by one question: do you want it to answer, or to do? For answers, the free Copilot app in Windows 11 or the ChatGPT and Claude desktop apps are enough. For hands-free control, Windows 11 has Voice access built in. For an assistant that carries out multi step jobs on your files and apps, you need an agent style assistant. You do not need a new Copilot+ PC or a powerful GPU for any of these, because they run their AI in the cloud. You only need serious hardware if you want to run AI models fully offline.

Search for an AI assistant for your PC and you get two kinds of results: lists of twenty chatbots, and spec sheets for new laptops with an AI chip. Neither answers the practical questions. Will it work on the PC I already own? What can it actually do once it is installed? And which one fits the way I work?

This guide answers those three questions in order. If you want a deeper breakdown of assistant categories, our guide to the five types of AI desktop assistant covers that ground. Here the focus is narrower: getting the right one running on a Windows PC.

Answer or do: the question that picks your assistant

Every AI assistant on a PC sits somewhere on a line between two jobs.

  • Answer assistants live in a window. You type or speak a question, they reply with text. They are great for writing, summarizing, explaining an error message or planning a trip. They do not touch your files or click anything unless you paste content in and copy their answer out.
  • Do assistants take actions. They open apps, find files, send the email, fill in the spreadsheet. Some do it one command at a time (Voice access: "click Save"). Others take a goal and work out the steps themselves ("find last month's invoices and total them").

Most people start with an answer assistant, then notice they spend half their time copying things in and out of it. That copying is the signal that a do assistant would save you real time.

Does your PC need an NPU or a GPU?

This is where a lot of buyers get misled. Marketing for new "AI PCs" can make it sound like you need new hardware to use an AI assistant at all. You do not. Here is how it actually breaks down.

What you wantHardware you need
Chat assistants (Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude)Any PC that runs a current browser or Windows 11. The AI runs on the provider's servers.
Voice control (Windows Voice access)Windows 11 version 22H2 or later and a microphone. Recognition runs on your PC after a one time language download.
Agent style assistantsAn ordinary PC and an internet connection. The heavy thinking happens in the cloud model.
Copilot+ PC exclusive featuresA Copilot+ PC with an NPU rated at 40 TOPS or more. Some Windows features, such as AI semantic file search, only run on these.
Running AI models fully offlinePlenty of RAM and ideally a recent graphics card. The bigger the model, the more memory it needs.

An NPU (neural processing unit) is a chip that runs small AI tasks efficiently on device. It matters for a handful of Windows features tied to Copilot+ PCs, which we cover in our look at Windows 11 AI file search. It does not make cloud assistants smarter or faster, because their work is not happening on your PC.

If you want an assistant that runs completely offline

Free tools such as Ollama and LM Studio let you download open models and run them on Windows with no internet connection. This is a real option if privacy rules forbid sending anything to the cloud. Be realistic about the trade off: small models that fit on a typical laptop are noticeably weaker than the large cloud models at reasoning and writing, and they run much faster with a dedicated graphics card that has its own memory. As a rule of thumb, try the smallest model first, see whether the answers are good enough for your work, and only then think about hardware.

Set it up properly in five steps

Whichever assistant you choose, these steps are the difference between something you use daily and something you forget about after a week.

  1. Fix the microphone first. In Windows, open Settings, then Privacy & security, then Microphone, and make sure both app access and desktop app access are on. A headset or a laptop mic in a quiet room beats a webcam mic across the desk.
  2. Give it one shortcut you will remember. Every assistant worth using has a keyboard shortcut or wake method. If you have to hunt for the window, you will stop using it. Pick a shortcut that does not clash with another app.
  3. Decide what it may touch. For any assistant that acts, start with a mode that asks before each action. Widen access once you trust it, and only for the folders and apps it needs.
  4. Connect only the apps you use. If the assistant can link to email, calendar or cloud storage, connect the two or three you live in, not everything on offer. Fewer connections means fewer surprises.
  5. Give it three real tasks on day one. Not "tell me a joke." Pick jobs you actually have: find a document you need, draft a reply you have been putting off, summarize a long PDF. You learn its strengths and limits in ten minutes.

Privacy checks before you install anything

An assistant on your PC can see more about you than one in a browser tab. Before installing one, check four things on the provider's own site:

  • Where your data goes. Is it processed on your PC, in the cloud, or both?
  • Whether conversations train models. Most major providers let you opt out. Find the setting and decide.
  • What it can access without asking. Files, screen, clipboard, connected accounts. You want clear switches for each.
  • How to undo. If it moves or renames files, is there a simple way to reverse it?

If you cannot find clear answers to these, that is your answer.

The bottom line

You do not need a new PC to get a useful AI assistant. Start by deciding whether you want answers or actions. For answers, install the free Copilot app or the ChatGPT or Claude desktop app. For hands-free control, switch on Voice access. For an assistant that finishes multi step jobs across your files and apps, add an agent style assistant and start it in a mode that asks before acting. Only buy hardware if you specifically want to run models offline, and test a small model first to see if that is really what you need.

FAQ

What is the best AI assistant for a PC?

It depends on whether you want answers or actions. For answers and writing help, the free Copilot app in Windows 11 or the ChatGPT and Claude desktop apps work well. For hands-free control, use Windows Voice access. For an assistant that completes multi step jobs on your files and apps, choose an agent style assistant such as Filect.

Do I need a Copilot+ PC or an NPU to use an AI assistant?

No. Popular assistants such as Copilot, ChatGPT and Claude run their AI in the cloud, so they work on an ordinary PC. An NPU rated at 40 TOPS or more is only required for certain Windows features reserved for Copilot+ PCs, such as AI semantic file search.

Is there a free AI assistant for Windows?

Yes. The Copilot app is free to use on Windows 11, ChatGPT and Claude both offer free tiers in their desktop apps, Voice access is built into Windows 11 at no cost, and tools such as Ollama and LM Studio let you run open models locally for free.

Can an AI assistant run on my PC without the internet?

Yes, with local model tools such as Ollama or LM Studio, which download open models and run them on your own hardware. Expect weaker results than large cloud models, and much faster performance with plenty of RAM and a dedicated graphics card.

Can an AI assistant control my PC by voice?

Yes. Windows Voice access lets you click, type and navigate by voice one command at a time. Agent style voice assistants such as Filect go further: you describe the goal, like finding a file or replying to an email, and the assistant carries out the steps.
