Private AI Assistant: What Stays Private and What Doesn't

November 28, 2026·7 min read

Quick answer

A private AI assistant is one where you know what leaves your computer, how long it is kept, and whether it trains anyone's models. The most private option runs fully on your own machine, with tools like LM Studio or Ollama, but local models mostly chat rather than act. Apple Intelligence keeps most work on the device and sends the rest to servers Apple says keep nothing. ChatGPT and Claude are cloud services, but both let you turn off training and use chats that are not saved. For an assistant that acts on your files and apps, the permissions you grant matter as much as where the model runs.

"Private" is one of the most used words in AI marketing and one of the least defined. One tool means "nothing leaves your laptop." Another means "we send it to a server but promise not to look." A third means "we do not train on it unless you leave a setting on." Those are very different promises, and the right one for you depends on what you plan to say to the assistant.

This guide breaks privacy into the questions that actually matter, sorts today's assistants into three tiers, and shows the exact settings that make the popular ones more private. Facts about other products come from their makers' own help and privacy pages, checked on November 28, 2026.

Five questions that define "private"

Ask these of any assistant before you trust it with anything sensitive.

  1. Where does the model run? On your computer, or on someone else's server.
  2. What gets sent? Your words only, or also audio, screenshots, files and contacts.
  3. How long is it kept? Not at all, 30 days for safety checks, or until you delete it.
  4. Is it used for training? And is that on by default, or something you opt into.
  5. What can it reach? An assistant that can read your inbox or move files has more to protect than one that only answers questions.

The first four are about the model. The fifth is about the assistant, and it gets more important the more an assistant can do. We cover it in its own section below.

Tier 1: Fully local

LM Studio is a desktop app that downloads open models and runs them on your own hardware, and its makers say it can run entirely offline. Ollama does the same from a command line and an API. Its privacy policy says it does not see your prompts when you run models locally, and it has a local only mode that switches off its cloud models and web search.

This is the right tier if a rule says data must not leave the building, or if you simply want certainty. The costs are real, though. Local models are smaller than the frontier cloud models, a laptop without a good GPU or Apple silicon will feel slow, and out of the box they answer questions rather than do things on your computer. Our explainer on local AI on your desktop covers the hardware side in more detail.

Tier 2: Private cloud

Apple Intelligence first checks whether a task can run on the device. When it cannot, it uses Private Cloud Compute. Apple says these servers receive only the data relevant to the request, that results are not retained after they are returned, and that your device will only talk to servers whose software has been publicly logged for inspection. You can also generate an Apple Intelligence report on your device that lists recent Private Cloud Compute requests.

That is a thoughtful design, but it is still Apple describing its own system, and it only applies on Apple devices that support Apple Intelligence.

Tier 3: Cloud with controls

Most people's assistant is ChatGPT or Claude, and both run in the cloud. What makes them more or less private is a handful of settings that many users never open.

How to make ChatGPT and Claude more private

ChatGPT

  1. Open Settings, then Data Controls, and turn off the model improvement setting if you do not want your chats used to train OpenAI's models.
  2. For anything sensitive, start a Temporary Chat. OpenAI says these stay out of your history and are not used to improve its models. A copy is kept for up to 30 days for safety and then deleted.
  3. Be aware that if you save a Temporary Chat, it becomes a normal chat and follows your model improvement setting.

Claude

  1. Click your name, then Settings, then Privacy, and set Help improve our AI models the way you want it.
  2. With it off, Anthropic's standard retention is 30 days. With it on, Anthropic says de-identified data may be kept for up to 5 years in its training pipelines.
  3. Use an Incognito chat for sensitive topics. Anthropic says these are not used to improve Claude even if the training setting is on.
  4. Turning training off later stops new chats from being used, but not ones already used in training. Set it before you share anything sensitive.

The same Claude setting covers Claude Code on a consumer account, which matters if you let an AI agent work in your projects.

The part most privacy guides skip: what the assistant can reach

A chatbot only knows what you paste into it. An assistant that controls your computer can open files, read your screen, draft email and change folders. That is far more useful, and it means "private" also has to cover what it is allowed to touch. Four habits keep that in check:

  • Grant the smallest access that works. Connect the one account the task needs, not every account you own.
  • Keep an approval step. Have the assistant ask before it sends, deletes or moves anything, at least until you trust it.
  • Exclude what it never needs. Tax records, medical folders and client archives can usually be kept out of reach entirely.
  • Prefer push to talk if always listening microphones bother you. Nothing is captured until you hold the key.

The bottom line

No assistant is private just because it says so. Ask where it runs, what it sends, how long it keeps it and whether it trains on it, then check what it can reach. Local tools give you certainty at the cost of capability. Cloud tools give you capability, and the settings in this guide claw back a good share of the privacy. Pick the tier that matches what you will actually say out loud.

FAQ

What is the most private AI assistant?

One that runs entirely on your own computer, such as an open model in LM Studio or Ollama with its cloud features turned off. Nothing you type leaves the machine. The trade off is that local models are smaller than the big cloud models and mostly answer rather than act.

Does ChatGPT or Claude train on my conversations?

On consumer accounts both give you a choice. ChatGPT has a model improvement setting in Data Controls, and Temporary Chats are not used to improve its models. Claude has a Help improve our AI models toggle under Settings, then Privacy, and Incognito chats are never used for training. Check both settings on your own account.

Is Apple Intelligence private?

Apple runs most requests on the device. Heavier ones go to Private Cloud Compute, which Apple says only receives data relevant to the request and does not retain it after the result is returned. Those are Apple's own statements, and Apple lets you generate a report of recent Private Cloud Compute requests on your device.

Does Filect send my files to the cloud?

Yes, for analysis. Filect sends file contents to OpenAI's enterprise API to understand them, and OpenAI's API terms say that data is not used to train its models. Filect does not store your files on its own servers, the search index stays on your computer, and you can exclude folders, drives or file types in the app settings.
