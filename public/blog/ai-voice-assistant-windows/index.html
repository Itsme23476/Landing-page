AI Voice Assistant for Windows: 4 Options Compared

November 22, 2026·7 min read

Quick answer

Windows no longer has one voice assistant. It has several, and each does a different job. Copilot Voice is the built in one for talking and asking questions. Voice access is the built in one for clicking and typing by voice. The ChatGPT desktop app is the strongest for open conversation. If you want an AI voice assistant for Windows that looks at your screen, answers out loud and then actually does the task, that is what Filect is built for. Pick by the job, not by the brand.

For years the answer to "what is the voice assistant on Windows" was one word: Cortana. Microsoft retired the standalone Cortana app in Windows in the spring of 2023, and nothing took its place as a single product. What you have now is a set of tools that each cover part of what people expect from a voice assistant.

This guide lays out the four realistic options, what each can and cannot do according to its own maker, and how to choose.

The four jobs a Windows voice assistant can do

Before comparing products, decide which of these you actually want. Most tools do one or two well.

  • Talk. Ask a question out loud and get a spoken answer, with natural back and forth.
  • See. Look at what is on your screen so you can ask "what does this error mean?" without describing it.
  • Type. Turn your speech into text in whatever app you are working in.
  • Act. Carry out the task: find the file, send the email, rename the folder, build the spreadsheet.

The last one is where the options differ most. Plenty of assistants on Windows will tell you how to do something. Very few will do it.

Option 1: Copilot Voice, the built in conversationalist

Copilot is the closest thing to a Cortana successor. You talk, it talks back, and it handles loose, natural questions.

To start it hands free you need to switch on the wake phrase, which Microsoft leaves off by default:

  1. Open the Microsoft Copilot app on your Windows desktop.
  2. Select Settings at the bottom of the left side menu.
  3. Select General.
  4. Turn on Listen for "Hey Copilot".

A few limits are worth knowing before you depend on it. Microsoft says the wake phrase only works while the Copilot app is running, will not start a new chat when the screen is locked or when another app is playing audio, and needs an internet connection to answer. The phrase itself is detected on your PC, and it only responds to "Hey Copilot" in English, although the chat can continue in other supported languages.

Copilot can also see. With Copilot Vision you share your desktop during a voice chat and ask about what is on it. Microsoft is direct about where that stops: Vision helps you understand what you are sharing, and it might highlight parts of the screen, but it will not click, type or scroll. So Copilot covers Talk and See, and leaves Act to you.

Pick it if you mostly want answers and explanations out loud and already pay for Microsoft 365.

Option 2: Voice access, the built in hands

Voice access is what Microsoft's Cortana retirement page recommends for controlling your PC. It is available in Windows 11, version 22H2 and later, and it uses on device speech recognition, so it works even without the internet.

It covers Type and a literal form of Act. You say "open Edge", "click Save", "scroll down", or dictate a paragraph, and it does exactly that. What it does not do is understand a goal. "Email the March report to Sam" means nothing to it; you would open the mail app, click New, dictate the address and attach the file, one command after another. It is dependable and already on your PC, but it is not an AI assistant in the sense most people mean.

Pick it if you need to operate Windows without a mouse or keyboard, especially for accessibility, and are happy to give step by step commands.

Option 3: The ChatGPT desktop app, for thinking out loud

The ChatGPT app for Windows includes voice. For talking an idea through or getting something explained while your hands are busy, it is one of the most natural voices to talk to. OpenAI's help page also says you can use voice in the desktop app to start tasks, check progress and coordinate agents through one conversation.

Its world is the ChatGPT app, though. OpenAI notes that Live voice does not support video or screen sharing, so it is not looking at the window in front of you while you talk. Voice allowances depend on your plan.

Pick it if conversation is the point and you already live in ChatGPT.

FAQ

Does Windows 11 have a built in AI voice assistant?

Yes, in two parts. The Copilot app has a voice chat you can start by saying "Hey Copilot" once you switch that setting on, and Voice access lets you click, scroll and type by voice. Copilot answers questions and Voice access follows exact commands. Neither one carries out a whole task for you from a spoken goal.

What replaced Cortana on Windows?

Microsoft retired the standalone Cortana app in Windows in the spring of 2023. Its support page points people to Voice access in Windows 11 for controlling the PC by voice and to Microsoft Copilot for questions and help.

Can an AI voice assistant see my screen on Windows?

Some can. Copilot Vision lets you share your desktop during a voice chat so Copilot can explain what is on it, though Microsoft says it will not click, type or scroll for you. Filect's hands free Assistant looks at your screen when you ask and can then act on what it sees, with your approval if you choose Ask first.

Is there a free AI voice assistant for Windows?

Voice access is built into Windows 11 at no extra cost, and Copilot voice chat is part of the Copilot app, although Copilot Vision needs a Microsoft 365 subscription or a work Copilot license.
