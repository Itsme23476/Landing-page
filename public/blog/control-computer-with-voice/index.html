How to Control Your Computer With Voice (Windows and Mac)

November 17, 2026·8 min read

Quick answer

There are three ways to control your computer with voice. Built in voice control (Voice access on Windows 11, Voice Control on Mac) is free and turns each spoken command into one click, scroll or keystroke. Dictation (Windows key plus H on Windows, the dictation key on Mac) replaces typing. An AI voice assistant takes a whole goal, like "find the March invoice and email it to Sam," and does the steps for you. If you need hands free access to every button, start with the built in tools. If you want to get work done faster by talking, add an AI assistant.

"Control my computer with my voice" means different things to different people. For someone with a wrist injury, it means never touching the mouse again. For someone who simply types slowly, it means speaking emails instead of typing them. For someone who has seen an AI agent work, it means saying what they want and watching it happen.

Each of those needs a different tool, and the wrong one is frustrating. This guide shows how to set up each method on Windows and Mac, what it is like to use day to day, and how to combine them.

Method 1: Built in voice control

Both Windows and macOS include a full voice control mode designed for people who cannot use a mouse or keyboard. It is the most complete way to operate every part of the screen by voice, and it costs nothing.

Windows 11: Voice access

  1. Open Settings, go to Accessibility, then Speech.
  2. Switch on Voice access. The first time, Windows downloads a speech model so recognition runs on your PC, and offers an interactive guide. Take the guide: it is short and teaches the core commands.
  3. A bar appears at the top of the screen showing whether it is listening. You can also start it with Windows key + Ctrl + S.

Commands worth learning first:

  • "Open Edge" or "switch to Outlook" to launch and change apps.
  • "Click Save" to press a button by its label.
  • "Show numbers" to put a number on every clickable item, then say the number. This is the command that makes awkward interfaces usable.
  • "Show grid" to divide the screen into numbered squares for precise clicks where there is no button.
  • "Scroll down," "press Enter," and plain speech to type into the focused field.
  • "What can I say" to open the full command list.

On Windows 10 the equivalent is the older Windows Speech Recognition, found under Ease of Access in Control Panel. Microsoft has deprecated it in favor of Voice access, so expect fewer improvements, but it still covers the basics of opening apps, clicking and dictating.

Mac: Voice Control

  1. Open System Settings, choose Accessibility, then Voice Control.
  2. Switch it on. A small microphone window appears on screen to show when it is listening.
  3. Open Commands in the same panel to see every built in command and to add your own.

The commands mirror Windows closely: "open Safari," "click Done," "show numbers," "show grid," "scroll down," and "what can I say." Custom commands are the hidden strength on Mac. You can map a phrase like "file it" to a keyboard shortcut or a short sequence of actions you repeat all day.

What it is like to live with

Voice control is precise and dependable, but it is literal. Replying to an email takes a sequence like "open Outlook," "click Reply," then the reply itself, then "click Send." Every step is yours to think of and say. For accessibility that is exactly right, because nothing happens that you did not ask for. For speed, it is usually slower than a mouse once you are past the first few commands. Expect a week of practice before it feels natural.

Method 2: Dictation

If what slows you down is typing, not clicking, dictation alone may be all you need.

  • Windows: click into any text field and press Windows key + H to start voice typing. Say "period," "comma," or "new line" for punctuation, or turn on automatic punctuation in its settings.
  • Mac: press the dictation key (the microphone key on recent keyboards) or the shortcut set under System Settings, Keyboard, Dictation.

Built in dictation is good for short messages. On long passages you will notice it writing down every "um" and occasionally misreading names. AI dictation tools clean up filler words and add punctuation as you go, which matters if you dictate for an hour a day.

Method 3: An AI voice assistant

The first two methods replace your hands. An AI voice assistant replaces some of the steps. Instead of telling the computer how, you tell it what:

  • "Find the lease agreement and tell me the notice period."
  • "Sort my Downloads folder into subfolders by type."
  • "Move my 3pm to Thursday and let the attendees know."
  • "Rename these scanned receipts with the date and the shop name."

With voice control, each of those is dozens of commands. With an AI assistant, it is one sentence, and the assistant does the searching, clicking or editing. Under the hood these tools act through your files, through commands, and through connections to apps like Gmail and Calendar; our explainer on AI that controls your computer covers how each approach works and where it breaks.

The trade off is trust. An assistant that can act can also act wrongly, so look for three things before you rely on one: an approval step before anything is sent or deleted, a visible record of each step it took, and clear limits on what it can reach. Start with copies of files and with drafts rather than sent emails.

Which method should you use?

  • You cannot use a mouse or keyboard, even briefly: built in voice control is the foundation. It reaches every button on screen. Add an AI assistant on top to cut down the number of commands you need.
  • You want to type less: start with dictation. Move to an AI dictation tool if you dictate a lot.
  • You want to get through work faster: an AI voice assistant, used alongside your mouse and keyboard rather than instead of them.
  • You want the Jarvis experience: that is an AI assistant that listens hands free and answers out loud. Our guide to Jarvis for PC compares the real options.

These methods do not compete. Many people run Voice access or Voice Control for navigation and an AI assistant for the bigger jobs.

Tips that make any voice method work better

  1. Use a decent microphone. A headset or a USB microphone close to your mouth beats a laptop's built in mic in a noisy room by a wide margin.
  2. Speak in phrases, not single words. Recognition improves with context. "Click the blue Save button" is easier to get right than "Save."
  3. Learn the escape hatch. In built in voice control that is "show numbers" or "show grid." In an AI assistant it is the stop or deny button.
  4. Teach it your words. Add client names, product names and jargon to the custom vocabulary so they stop coming out wrong.
  5. Check privacy settings once. Know whether your tool listens for a wake word or only when you press a key, and what audio it sends anywhere.

FAQ

Can I control my whole computer with my voice?

Yes. Windows 11 Voice access and macOS Voice Control let you open apps, click, scroll and type entirely by voice, for free. They follow literal commands one step at a time. An AI voice assistant lets you describe the result you want instead and works out the steps.

How do I turn on voice control in Windows 11?

Open Settings, go to Accessibility, then Speech, and switch on Voice access. The first time, Windows downloads a speech model and offers a short tutorial. After that you can start it from the same setting or with the keyboard shortcut Windows key plus Ctrl plus S.

How do I control my Mac with my voice?

Open System Settings, choose Accessibility, then Voice Control, and switch it on. A microphone icon appears on screen. Say "show numbers" to label everything clickable, then say a number to click it. Say "what can I say" to see the full command list.

What is the difference between voice control and an AI voice assistant?

Voice control turns each spoken command into one action, such as click, scroll or type. An AI voice assistant takes a goal, such as find last month's invoice and email it to Sam, and carries out the steps for you, often with an approval before anything is sent.
