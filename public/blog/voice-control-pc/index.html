Voice Control PC: The Complete Windows Voice Access Guide

November 18, 2026·8 min read

Quick answer

To voice control your PC, open Settings, go to Accessibility, then Speech, and switch on Voice access (Windows 11, version 22H2 or later). It is free and, once its language files are downloaded, runs on your PC without an internet connection. Learn five commands first: "open" an app, "click" a button by name, "show numbers", "scroll down", and "what can I say". Press Alt + Shift + B to wake it or put it to sleep. On Windows 10 you get the older Windows Speech Recognition instead.

Most guides to voice controlling a PC stop at "turn it on and say click." That gets you started, but it is not what makes voice control usable for a full workday. What does is knowing the handful of commands that rescue you when an app has no labels, setting up shortcuts for the things you repeat, and knowing what to check when Windows suddenly stops listening.

This guide is Windows only and goes deeper than our general walkthrough of how to control your computer with voice, which also covers Mac.

Step 1: Set up Voice access properly

  1. Check your version. Open Settings, then System, then About. Voice access needs Windows 11 version 22H2 or later. If you are on Windows 10, skip to the Windows 10 section below.
  2. Allow the microphone. In Settings, go to Privacy & security, then Microphone, and make sure both "Let apps access your microphone" and "Let desktop apps access your microphone" are on. This is the most common reason Voice access appears to do nothing.
  3. Turn it on. Go to Settings, Accessibility, Speech and switch on Voice access. You can also type "voice access" into Windows search and open it from there.
  4. Download the language files. The first launch needs an internet connection to fetch the speech model for your language. After that, recognition happens on your PC.
  5. Pick your microphone and do the guide. Voice access offers an interactive tutorial.
  6. Decide on startup. If you rely on voice, tick the option to start Voice access before or after you sign in to your PC, so it is ready without touching the keyboard.

A bar sits at the top of the screen showing whether Voice access is listening, asleep, or has its microphone off. Most "it is not hearing me" moments are simply that it is asleep.

Numbers and grid: the two commands that unlock everything

"Click Save" only works when the button has a readable label. Plenty of apps have icon only buttons, custom toolbars or web pages built in ways Windows cannot read. That is where "show numbers" comes in: it draws a number on every clickable item it can find, and you say the number. Say "show numbers on taskbar" or "show numbers here" to narrow it down, and "hide numbers" or "cancel" to clear it.

When even that fails, "show grid" splits the screen into nine numbered squares. Say a number to zoom into that square, then again to zoom further, until the pointer sits on the exact spot. Once you know a location you can say it in one go, such as "mouse grid 5 7 3." To drag, say "mark" on the item you want to move, move the grid to the destination, and say "drag." With more than one monitor, each display gets a letter, so "B" moves the grid to your second screen.

Typing and fixing text by voice

In the default mode, anything that is not a command gets typed into the focused text box. The editing commands are what make this practical:

  • Select: "Select that," "Select line," "Select from dear to regards."
  • Remove: "Delete that," "Scratch that," "Delete previous word."
  • Fix a misheard word: "Correct that" shows alternatives. "Spell that" lets you spell it letter by letter.
  • Punctuation and layout: "Period," "Comma," "Question mark," "New line," "New paragraph."
  • Teach it names: "Add to vocabulary" stores words it keeps getting wrong, such as client or product names.

If a phrase you want to type happens to be a command, say "type" before it. If you are writing a long piece and Voice access keeps treating sentences as commands, say "dictation mode". Say "default mode" to switch back.

Step 3: Make your own voice shortcuts

This is the feature most people never find. Voice access lets you create voice shortcuts: a phrase you pick that runs a sequence of up to eight actions. Actions include opening an app, file, folder or website, pasting a block of text, pressing keys, clicking, and waiting between steps. Voice shortcuts are currently available in English only.

Good candidates are the things you do many times a day:

  • "Start my day" opens your mail, calendar and the project folder you work in.
  • "Sign off" pastes your standard email closing.
  • "Clients" opens the shared drive folder you use most.

Create them from the Voice access settings, or by voice, starting with "click create new shortcut." Keep phrases short and unlike anything you would normally dictate, so they do not fire by accident.

When Voice access stops listening

  1. It is asleep. Check the bar at the top. Say "voice access wake up" or press Alt + Shift + B.
  2. The mic is muted. Alt + Shift + C toggles the microphone. A hardware mute key on a headset or laptop also counts.
  3. Wrong microphone. If you plug in a headset after launch, choose it in Voice access settings. Windows does not always switch for you.
  4. Desktop apps are blocked. Revisit the two microphone switches under Privacy & security.
  5. It hears, but gets words wrong. Move the mic closer, cut background noise, speak in full phrases rather than single words, and add stubborn names with "add to vocabulary."
  6. Your language is not offered. Voice access supports a set list of languages, including several English variants, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Chinese and Japanese. Change it under Voice access settings, Languages.

Voice control on Windows 10

Windows 10 does not get Voice access. It has Windows Speech Recognition, which you set up from Control Panel under Ease of Access, then Speech Recognition. It handles opening apps, clicking named items, a numbered overlay ("show numbers"), a mouse grid ("mousegrid") and dictation. Microsoft has deprecated it in favor of Voice access, so do not expect improvements. If voice is central to how you use your PC, upgrading to Windows 11 is the single biggest improvement you can make.

Where built in voice control runs out

Voice access is excellent at one thing: turning every spoken instruction into exactly one action. That makes it reliable and the right foundation for anyone who cannot use a mouse or keyboard. It also means you have to think through every step. Replying to an email with an attachment is a dozen commands: open the app, find the message, click reply, dictate, open the attach dialog, browse to the folder, pick the file, send.

An AI voice assistant works at a different level. You say the goal, "reply to Sam with the signed contract attached," and it handles the steps. The two are not rivals. A common setup is Voice access for navigation and an AI assistant for anything that would take more than a few commands. FAQ

Can I fully control my PC with my voice?

Yes. Voice access in Windows 11 (version 22H2 and later) lets you open apps, click, scroll, drag, press keys and type using only your voice, and it is free. After a one time download of language files, recognition runs on your PC without an internet connection.

What is the shortcut to start and stop Voice access listening?

Alt plus Shift plus B wakes Voice access up or puts it to sleep, and Alt plus Shift plus C turns the microphone off or on. By voice, say "voice access wake up" or "voice access sleep".

Does Windows 10 have voice control?

Windows 10 does not have Voice access. It has the older Windows Speech Recognition, found in Control Panel under Ease of Access. Microsoft has deprecated it in favor of Voice access, so it still works for basic commands and dictation but is not being improved.

Can I create custom voice commands on Windows?

Yes. Voice access supports voice shortcuts: a phrase you choose that runs up to eight actions, such as opening an app, file, folder or website, pasting text, pressing keys, clicking and waiting. Voice shortcuts are currently available in English only.

Is there a way to voice control a PC by describing a goal instead of each step?

Yes, with an AI voice assistant. Instead of saying each click, you say the result you want, such as find the signed lease and tell me the notice period, and the assistant carries out the steps. It works alongside Voice access rather than replacing it.
